Soil Temperature Guide for Lawn Care

Soil temperature is the most reliable indicator for timing lawn care applications. Unlike air temperature or calendar date, soil temperature tells you exactly what is happening at the root zone — pre-emergent germination, root activity, and grass growth are all driven by soil temperature, not the forecast high.

This tracker shows current readings at three depths: surface (0 cm), approximately 2 inches (6 cm), and approximately 6 inches (18 cm). The 2-inch reading is most relevant for lawn care decisions — it reflects the rooting zone of warm-season grasses and the germination zone of most annual weeds. If you also want to track heat accumulation over the season, see the GDD Calculator.

Bermuda Grass Soil Temperature Windows

Bermuda grass (Cynodon dactylon) is dormant below 50°F. The pre-emergent crabgrass window opens at 50–55°F. Light nitrogen is appropriate at first visible greenup (55–65°F). A full fertilizer, herbicide, and PGR program is appropriate above 65°F with confirmed canopy coverage. Above 80°F, avoid elemental sulfur and drought-stress applications.

Zoysia Grass Soil Temperature

Zoysia breaks dormancy later than bermuda. Hold all inputs until 2-inch soil consistently exceeds 55°F. The pre-emergent window closes around 65°F. Confirm visual greenup before applying nitrogen — zoysia frequently shows adequate soil temperature before visibly breaking green. Full program including PGR is appropriate above 65°F with confirmed growth.

St. Augustine Soil Temperature

St. Augustine requires soil above 60°F before nitrogen applications. The pre-emergent window is active through 70°F. St. Augustine is more sensitive to cold damage than bermuda — be conservative at borderline temperatures. Most St. Augustine programs do not include PGR. Watch for chinch bug pressure as soil temperatures climb past 75°F.

Cool-Season Grass Soil Temperature

Cool-season grasses (tall fescue, Kentucky bluegrass, perennial ryegrass) thrive when soil is 50–65°F — the ideal window for fertilization and overseeding. Above 75°F, cool-season grasses enter summer stress. Above 80°F most varieties are dormant or declining. The best growth window is spring and fall.

Frequently Asked Questions

What soil temperature does bermuda grass need to break dormancy?

Bermuda grass begins breaking dormancy when 2-inch soil temperature consistently reaches 50°F. Visible greenup typically begins between 55–65°F. You can resume light nitrogen once you see green shoots, and a full fertilizer and PGR program is appropriate above 65°F.

What soil temperature should I use for pre-emergent timing?

Apply crabgrass pre-emergent when 2-inch soil temperature reaches 50–55°F for 3–5 consecutive days. Applying after soil consistently exceeds 55°F risks missing the window for summer annual weeds like crabgrass and goosegrass.

What is the ideal soil temperature for zoysia grass?

Zoysia breaks dormancy when 2-inch soil reaches 55–65°F, slightly later than bermuda. Apply pre-emergent before soil hits 65°F. Confirm visual greenup before applying nitrogen — zoysia often shows adequate soil temp before the canopy is visibly green.

What soil temperature is too hot for lawn applications?

Above 80°F, avoid elemental sulfur (burn risk increases significantly) and high-rate nitrogen applications under drought conditions. St. Augustine is heat-sensitive above 85°F. Cool-season grasses are typically stressed or dormant above 75°F soil temperature.
