Soil Temperature Guide for Lawn Care

Soil temperature is the most reliable indicator for timing lawn care applications. Unlike air temperature or calendar date, soil temperature tells you exactly what is happening at the root zone where it matters — pre-emergent germination, root activity, and grass growth are all driven by soil temperature, not the forecast high.

This tracker shows current soil temperature at three depths: surface (0 cm), approximately 2 inches (6 cm), and approximately 6 inches (18 cm). The 2-inch reading is the most relevant for most lawn care decisions — it reflects the rooting zone of warm-season grasses and the germination zone of most annual weeds.

Bermuda Grass Soil Temperature Windows

Bermuda grass (Cynodon dactylon) is dormant below 50°F soil temperature. The pre-emergent crabgrass window opens when 2-inch soil reaches 50–55°F. Light nitrogen can be applied at first visible greenup around 55–65°F. A full fertilizer, herbicide, and PGR program is appropriate above 65°F with confirmed canopy coverage. Above 80°F, avoid elemental sulfur and drought-stress-inducing applications.

Zoysia Grass Soil Temperature

Zoysia breaks dormancy later than bermuda. Hold all inputs until 2-inch soil consistently exceeds 55°F. The pre-emergent window closes around 65°F. Confirm visual greenup before applying nitrogen — zoysia frequently shows low soil temperature but is not yet visibly green. Full program including PGR is appropriate above 65°F with confirmed growth.

St. Augustine Soil Temperature

St. Augustine requires soil above 60°F before nitrogen applications. The pre-emergent window is active through 70°F. St. Augustine is more sensitive to cold damage than bermuda — be conservative when soil temperatures are borderline. Most St. Augustine programs do not include PGR. Watch for chinch bug pressure as soil temperatures climb past 75°F.

Cool-Season Grass Soil Temperature

Cool-season grasses (tall fescue, Kentucky bluegrass, perennial ryegrass) thrive when soil is between 50–65°F. This is the ideal window for fertilization and overseeding. Above 75°F, cool-season grasses enter summer stress. Above 80°F, most cool-season varieties are dormant or declining. The best growth window is spring and fall when soil temperatures are in the 50–65°F range.
