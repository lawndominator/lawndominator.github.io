Seeding guide

Overseeding guide

Overseeding means putting seed into a lawn that is already there. On tall fescue and bluegrass it is how you thicken a thin stand. On Bermuda it is a winter-color choice, usually perennial ryegrass, and it is a different job with a different ending.

Decide which overseeding you mean

Cool-season overseeding repairs tall fescue, Kentucky bluegrass, fine fescue, or ryegrass. The goal is a thicker stand of the same grass, timed for early fall, with the new plants still there in five years.

Bermuda overseeding paints a dormant warm-season lawn green for the winter with perennial ryegrass, sometimes annual ryegrass. The rye is a temporary crop. Virginia Tech’s warm-season calendar recommends it only for Bermuda, at 5 to 10 pounds of pure live seed per 1,000 square feet when you want winter color, and it says not to overseed zoysia, centipede, or St. Augustine because the rye competes too hard in spring. If you do not need winter color, skip it. Dormant Bermuda is supposed to be brown.

Those two projects fail in opposite ways. People seed fescue in May and then spray a pre-emergent over it. People seed rye into Bermuda and then also apply a fall pre-emergent for Poa annua, which kills the rye before it starts. Pick one plan.

Cool-season timing and seedbed

Late summer through early fall is the window. Soil is warm, nights are cooling, and crabgrass is finishing rather than germinating into every gap. University of Minnesota Extension treats this season as the one when cool-season grasses store reserves and actually want to grow. Seeding into hot soil in July, or into cold soil in April while you are also trying to stop crabgrass, is the expensive way to learn that.

A missed early fall is sometimes salvaged with dormant seeding: seed spread late enough that it will not germinate until spring. Minnesota’s calendar discusses that late-fall option. It is a second choice. Seed sitting on frozen ground all winter is bird food and washout risk, and spring seedlings then have to survive summer with shallow roots.

Seed-to-soil contact matters more than the brand on the bag. Mow at the regular height. Rake or verticut enough to open the canopy. Core-aerate if the lawn is compacted, and leave the cores broken up on the surface so they become topdressing. Spread seed, then drag or rake it into the slits. A light compost or soil topdressing helps on thin spots. Do not bury seed under an inch of anything. It needs light and moisture, not a grave.

Rate it for the job. NC State’s tall fescue establishment rate, which this site’s seed calculator uses, is 6 pounds per 1,000 square feet for a new stand. Overseeding existing turf is lighter than that, because you are filling gaps, not building a lawn from zero. NC State has also warned that too much tall fescue seed makes a crowded, weak stand that handles heat and disease poorly. Kentucky bluegrass is seeded much lighter than tall fescue and comes up slower. Perennial ryegrass comes up fast and is easy to overdo. Use the calculator, then read the seed tag. Buy by pure live seed, not by the weight of the coating.

Match the seed to the lawn you have. Throwing Kentucky bluegrass into a full-sun tall fescue lawn in the transition zone gives you two grasses with two different needs. In dense shade, a fine fescue belongs in the mix and bluegrass mostly does not. If more than about half the lawn is dead, you are renovating, not overseeding, and the rate and the soil prep both go up.

Water, mow, and the products that will ruin it

Until the seed roots, the top quarter-inch of soil stays damp. That can mean two or three light waterings a day for the first week or two, then a shift to deeper, less frequent water once you can tug a seedling and feel resistance. Letting it dry out for an afternoon at day six is a common way to lose the stand. So is drowning it in a puddle.

Mow when the existing grass needs it, with a sharp blade, and do not scalp the seedlings. Wait to mow brand-new seedlings until they are tall enough that you are only taking the top third. Keep traffic off the soft spots.

Most pre-emergent herbicides will stop this seed the same way they stop crabgrass. If you applied one in spring, read the reseeding interval on that label before you buy seed. It is often a couple of months, and sometimes longer for prodiamine. Do not apply a fall pre-emergent for Poa annua on a lawn you just seeded. A starter fertilizer is appropriate when the seedlings are up and the soil test does not already show plenty of phosphorus. “Starter” is not a license to exceed the nitrogen rate. Weed-and-feed is the wrong product on new seedlings. Broadleaf herbicides can injure young grass. Wait until the new plants have been mowed several times and the label says the turf is old enough.

Ryegrass on dormant Bermuda

Virginia Tech’s figure for winter color is 5 to 10 pounds of pure live ryegrass seed per 1,000 square feet. Perennial ryegrass is the usual choice when you want a finer winter lawn. Annual ryegrass is coarser, cheaper, and easier to transition off in spring, which is why some people still use it. Intermediate ryegrass shows up in sports-field mixes. For a home lawn, perennial rye at the low end of that range is plenty if the Bermuda was healthy. The high end is for thin cover or heavy use.

Timing is when Bermuda is slowing and the seed can germinate into a thinning canopy, before a hard freeze, and while you can still mow the Bermuda short. Mow the Bermuda down, rake up the clippings and excess thatch, and get the rye seed onto soil. Many people do this as nights start to cool and daytime highs are falling out of the 80s. If you wait until the Bermuda is already straw and the soil is cold, germination drags and the stand stays thin. If you seed while Bermuda is still growing hard, the Bermuda shades the seedlings out.

Do not apply a fall pre-emergent if you want this rye. Virginia Tech flags that directly: a pre-emergent aimed at annual bluegrass and winter broadleaves will also hit the ryegrass. You are choosing winter color or you are choosing Poa prevention. You do not get both from a standard pre-emergent.

Once the Bermuda is dormant, the rye is the lawn you are maintaining. Virginia Tech allows up to about 0.7 pound of readily available nitrogen per 1,000 square feet per active growing month on a ryegrass overseeding. That nitrogen is for the rye, during months it is actually growing, not a reason to feed in a freeze. Mow the rye at a cool-season height. Water it. It will need both all winter in the South, because the brown Bermuda underneath is not the plant using that water.

Spring transition, and when to skip the rye

The bill for winter color comes due at green-up. Ryegrass that is still thick in May shades the Bermuda and slows the only lawn you meant to keep. Virginia Tech’s athletic-field note says to remove the overseeding, chemically or culturally, as soon as the spring playing season is over, specifically so the Bermuda can recover. On a home lawn the cultural version is: stop feeding the rye, mow it lower as the weather warms, and let the Bermuda have the light. A chemical removal is a label-specific herbicide that hurts rye more than Bermuda. It is easy to get wrong on a lawn that is not fully dormant-tolerant, so read a product made for that transition and do not invent a glyphosate rate.

Zoysia and St. Augustine should not get this treatment. Virginia Tech says the spring competition is too hard on those grasses. If a zoysia lawn is thin, the fix is patience, soil, and zoysia plugs or sod in early summer, not a winter rye crop.

If you like the tan look of dormant Bermuda, overseeding is optional work and optional water. Spend the fall on leaves, mowing until growth stops, and the winter weed plan instead.

Questions people ask

When should I overseed a tall fescue or bluegrass lawn?

Late summer to early fall, when the soil is still warm and the worst heat has passed. Keep the seedbed moist until the seedlings root. Dormant seeding in late fall is a backup, not the better window.

How much ryegrass do I put on Bermuda?

Virginia Tech recommends 5 to 10 pounds of pure live ryegrass seed per 1,000 square feet for winter color on Bermuda. It does not recommend overseeding zoysia or St. Augustine.

Can I use pre-emergent when I overseed?

Not a pre-emergent that stops grass seed. Fall products aimed at Poa annua will stop ryegrass and fescue too. Check the reseeding interval if you already applied one in spring.

Should I fertilize an overseeded Bermuda lawn in winter?

Once Bermuda is dormant, a light nitrogen application is for the ryegrass that is actually growing. Virginia Tech’s ceiling for readily available nitrogen on an overseeding is about 0.7 pound per 1,000 square feet in an active growing month. Do not feed frozen turf.

How do I get the ryegrass out in spring?

Stop feeding it, mow lower as Bermuda wakes up, and give the Bermuda the light. Chemical removal is a separate, label-specific step used on sports fields. Do not spray a non-selective herbicide on a lawn that is greening up.
